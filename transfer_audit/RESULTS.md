# Structure-matched transfer audit: results and claim boundary

Analysis date: 2026-10-05 (Asia/Shanghai)  
Protocol: `structure_matched_transfer_exploratory_2026_10_05`  
Status: local exploratory audit; **not a confirmatory validation and not a biological accuracy study**.

## Cohort and execution

The frozen ChEMBL source frame contained 4,225 records. After applying the original eligibility rules and excluding the prior 49 tasks, 44 C/J/N-stratified tasks remained. Forty had an unambiguous one-to-one structure match to the 2021 DrugCentral structure crosswalk (18 ATC-J and 22 ATC-N); four were excluded solely for lack of a unique crosswalk. No activity, label-coverage, or ranking result was used to select the 40.

All 40/40 tasks completed ChEMBL activity, target-metadata, and PubMed retrieval with no recorded retrieval error. The unchanged adapter generated 76,958 evidence objects from 16,168 activity observations and 56,698 retrieved PubMed records. Counts are nested and highly dependent—not independent observations. At the drug-task level, 36/40 were rankable; 4 had no eligible candidate target. Among rankable tasks, the median candidate universe was 7.5 (range 1–154). Only 364 measured objects were eligible under the frozen rules; 15,804 were excluded. There were 1,050 literature-only screening objects, 31,040 records requiring manual review, and 28,700 excluded literature objects. These results expose substantial evidence attrition and missingness rather than disguising it.

## Did the workflow change prioritization?

Across the 36 rankable tasks, membership in the first min(10, N) candidates (where N is the task's candidate-universe size) differed between evidence-gated and ungated-union rankings in 6/36 tasks (16.7%); the median replacement count was 0 (range 0–4; median Jaccard 1.00). The corresponding count versus the measured-priority comparator was 9/36 (25.0%; median replacements 0, range 0–9; median Jaccard 1.00). Because 22 of the 36 rankable tasks had fewer than 10 candidates, these pooled counts describe top-k changes and are not full Top-10 turnover rates. Among the 14 tasks with at least 10 candidates, full Top-10 membership changed in 6/14 versus ungated-union and 9/14 versus measured-priority ordering. Thus the workflow changed some candidate lists under the declared rules; this cohort does not show frequent or large changes in the pooled top-k lists. The 5.0-to-5.5 threshold change altered no first-min(10, N) lists; the 6.0 threshold altered one. Threshold sensitivity in this extension was modest.

These counts use the single-compound adapter's fixed union of eligible measured and literature-supported targets for all three rankings. Its measured-only comparator ranks that shared universe using measured status and potency; targets without measured values remain in the list and are alphabetically ordered after measured targets. This differs from the formula-level comparison, where the measured-only ranking had a narrower candidate universe. The two cohorts' measured-only change rates are therefore not directly comparable.

Task-level inspection shows a heterogeneous pattern rather than a cohort-wide effect. Relative to ungated ranking, changes ranged from one replacement in CHEMBL2023898 and CHEMBL218394 to four in CHEMBL231813 (Jaccard 0.429). Relative to measured-only, CHEMBL40 had the largest change: 9/10 Top-10 entries differed (Jaccard 0.053) in a 79-candidate universe with zero eligible measured-supported targets and 79 literature-supported targets. This is consistent with evidence-source composition driving the declared ranking response. It does not show that the gated list is biologically better. See `DECISION_IMPACT.md` for task-level examples and the claim boundary.

Stratifying the 36 rankable tasks by measured-data availability clarifies where the comparisons are informative. Twenty-four tasks had at least one eligible measured-supported target. In these tasks, membership in the first min(10, N) candidates changed in 6/24 (25.0%) versus ungated and 7/24 (29.2%) versus fixed-universe measured-priority ordering; 10 of these tasks had at least 10 candidates, and all observed changes occurred among those 10 full-size lists (6/10 and 7/10, respectively). In the 12 tasks without any eligible measured target, gated and ungated top-k lists were identical (0/12 changed); eight had fewer than 10 candidates. The measured-priority comparator in this stratum contains no measured scores and therefore resolves all ordering alphabetically; it changed in 2/12 top-k lists, both among the four full-size lists. This contrast should not be interpreted as a comparison against an informative measured-evidence model. These subgroup summaries are descriptive and were not prespecified as confirmatory endpoints.

For context only, pooling this cohort with the three earlier post hoc cohorts gives 89 selected tasks, 77 rankable, and 12 non-rankable. Across those 77 rankable tasks, membership in the first min(10, N) candidates changed in 10 tasks (13.0%; median replacements 0, range 0–4) versus ungated and in 18 (23.4%; median replacements 0, range 0–9) versus measured-only. The pooled median candidate universe was 6, and 49/77 candidate sets had fewer than 10 members; all observed changes occurred among the other 28 full-size sets. These accumulated top-k summaries are descriptive and must not be interpreted as full Top-10 turnover rates for all 77 tasks. The cohorts were added sequentially after earlier results were inspected, so 89/77 are transparent accumulated workflow counts—not a prospectively fixed validation sample or independent replications.

A descriptive cross-task source-overlap audit across the 89 accumulated tasks found that 2,436 of 3,916 task pairs (62.2%) shared at least one PMID, ChEMBL document, or assay identifier. The pair counts for each source class were 509, 2,287, and 2,221, respectively; source classes overlap. All 89 tasks were in one connected component. This describes source dependence and is not an effective-sample-size estimate; the task count must not be used as 89 independent replicates or as a basis for naive task-level significance tests. The audit output and SHA-256 hashes of all four input evidence-object files are recorded in `cross_task_dependence_89.json`.

## A useful diagnostic result—and why it is not predictive validation

The post-selection, exact-structure-linked DrugCentral/FDA-label audit yielded 21 unique historical drug–target pairs across 9 tasks. Fourteen pairs (66.7%) were already represented by an eligible same-task ChEMBL measured drug–gene input; the remaining 7 pairs (33.3%) were absent from the ranked candidate universes. All 14 input-overlapping pairs appeared in the Top 10, while none of the 7 absent pairs could be ranked. A naive reading would call this 14/21 (66.7%) Top-10 label coverage. The provenance audit shows that the entire apparent Top-10 agreement is confined to relations already represented in the measured input; it is not an independent test of target prediction. This is a concrete use of the workflow's provenance/coverage controls: it detects circularity risk and separates ranking from candidate-generation failure.

Among the 14 already-input-supported pairs, the median rank was 2 for evidence-gated, 3 for ungated-union, and 2 for measured-only. Top-10 pair coverage was identical for all three methods (14/21 overall). The evidence gate therefore did not improve Top-10 label coverage over measured-only. DrugCentral integrates multiple upstream evidence sources, so the 7 pairs without exact same-task ChEMBL support are not proven independent either. No accuracy, recall, superiority, or clinical-mechanism claim is justified.

## Reproducibility check

Rebuilding the evidence objects and ranks from saved raw responses reproduced all 76,958 evidence objects, every feature object and ranking list, and all 40 task-level decision audits exactly. As a metamorphic label-leakage check, removing the historical FDA-label target field from task metadata and recomputing left all ranks, features, and task-level audits unchanged. This verifies that the reference labels are not an input to the implemented ranking calculation; it does not exclude shared upstream source dependence or prove that cohort selection was independent of every external label. These checks support deterministic, auditable execution on the additional cohort, not biological validity.

## Implication for the manuscript

This audit is useful as a bounded exploratory transfer/diagnostic supplement if the manuscript needs evidence beyond one formula cohort. The defensible claim is: the workflow transferred to 40 additional structure-resolved tasks, was fully retrievable, produced reproducible ranks for 36, and flagged that apparent FDA-label agreement was wholly explained by already-present measured relations while one third of label pairs were outside the candidate universe. Task-level results show mostly unchanged Top-10s with a small number of large, traceable differences. Do **not** present the 14/21 as validation performance, and do not claim the evidence gate outperforms measured-only. Keep this result separate from the frozen 14-formula benchmark and the scheduled blinded human review.

## Important process deviation

An initial 60-task cohort draft omitted original approval-year and ATC-stratum rules. No rankings were calculated for that draft. Raw activity and PubMed aggregate response files exist for five tasks and an additional activity response exists for a sixth; target-metadata completeness was not certified. These files are retained under `runtime_invalidated_60task_selection/`, excluded from this report, and not pooled with the corrected 40-task cohort.
